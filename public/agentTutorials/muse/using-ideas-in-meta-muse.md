# Using Ideas in Meta Muse

Ideas is the tab where Muse pitches *you*. Instead of waiting for instructions, Muse looks at your conversations, your projects, and the things you've left half-finished — and suggests concrete things it could build or do for you next.

Each suggestion shows up as a card: a short title, a plain-language description of what Muse would actually do, and a way to kick it off. See one you like, tap it, and Muse gets to work. See one you don't, dismiss it and move on.

It's the closest thing in the app to having an assistant who pays attention and comes to you with proposals — "I noticed this, want me to handle it?" Here's how to use it well.

## What Ideas Are — and What They Aren't

Ideas are proactive suggestions generated by Muse, based on your conversations and context. They describe real work Muse is offering to do: finish a guide you commissioned, wire up a feature in your app, fill out an application before a deadline, check something for you.

A few distinctions that keep things clear:

- **Ideas are proposals, not assignments.** Nothing happens until you pick one. Muse suggests; you decide.
- **They're not a to-do list you write.** You don't create idea cards yourself — Muse generates them from what it knows about you.
- **They're not Goals.** A Goal is an ongoing objective Muse helps you track over time. An Idea is a single piece of work on offer right now.
- **They expire.** Cards retire on their own after about two weeks, so the tab stays fresh instead of filling up with stale suggestions.

## What Ideas Can Do

Here's what that looks like in practice:

- **Pick up your unfinished business** — "I can finish your commissioned guide" or "want me to check the state of that run you stopped?" Muse notices loose ends and offers to tie them off.
- **Propose work you haven't thought of** — money-back claims on price drops, unclaimed-property checks, setup scripts for a new laptop. Things that are genuinely useful but would never occur to you to ask for.
- **Bundle multi-step work into one tap** — instead of five separate requests, one card describes the whole job: research, build, verify, done.
- **Keep the list fresh** — cards you ignore quietly age out after about two weeks, so you're never staring at a graveyard of old suggestions.

## Opening the Ideas Tab

1. Open the Muse app on your phone, or go to [muse.ai](https://muse.ai) in your browser.
2. Look for the **Ideas** tab in the main navigation — its icon is a lightbulb.
3. Tap it. You'll see your idea cards, each with an icon, a bold title, and a short description of what Muse would do. Some are grouped under category headings like **Financial Management**.

## How Ideas Get Created

You don't write ideas — Muse does. It generates them from your recent conversations, your goals, and the context it has about your projects. That's why the cards often reference things you've actually talked about: a guide you commissioned, an app you're building, a scholarship deadline you mentioned.

Each card tells you three things:

1. **What** Muse is offering — the bold title, like "Get your new MacBook fully working in one pass."
2. **How** it would do it — the description underneath, in plain language: what gets installed, moved, or checked, and what your part is (often just "you run it once" or "you review and submit").
3. **Why now** — many cards reference a deadline or an open thread, so you can tell at a glance whether it's timely.

Because they're generated from your context, the more Muse knows about your projects, the better the suggestions get. Someone who talks through their work in chat will see sharper ideas than someone who barely chats at all.

## Running an Idea

When a card looks good, it's worth opening first. Tapping a card brings up its **detail view** — the full pitch behind the one-line summary:

- **The description** — the complete version of what Muse is offering, in plain language.
- **What's included** — a checklist of the actual deliverables (a setup script, a workload guide, a verification check), each with a one-line explanation of what it is.
- **How it works** — a short paragraph on the plan: what Muse builds, what your part is (often just running something once, or reviewing and submitting), and how you'll confirm it's done.
- A blue **"Let's do it"** button to kick the whole thing off.

> **📸 Screenshot:** An idea's detail view, showing the full description, what's included, and how it works.

![An idea detail view in Meta Muse](/agentTutorials/muse/images/ideaindepth.png)

To run it, hit **"Let's do it"**. What happens next depends on your platform:

- **On Android**, an idea card sent into chat can run in place and show live progress as Muse works.
- **On iPhone**, tapping a card opens the idea's detail view instead — it doesn't run inline.
- **On the web**, the card itself carries the build action, labeled **"Let's do it"**.

One more place ideas show up: some posts in your **Feed** are built from ideas, and those posts carry the same build action on every platform — so you can kick off the work straight from a Feed post too.

## Dismissing Ideas — and Why They Expire

Not every suggestion will be a winner. To get rid of one:

1. **Long-press** the idea card.
2. Choose the **dismiss** control when it appears.

That's the only way — and it's yours to do, not Muse's. If you ask in chat to "dismiss that idea," Muse can't do it for you; there's no way for the assistant to remove a card. Long-press it yourself.

Cards also disappear on their own. Every idea has an expiry, and cards stop being shown after about two weeks. So if you open the tab and a card you were meaning to try is gone, it probably just aged out rather than being deleted — and if the work still matters, just ask Muse for it in chat.

## Tips for Getting Better Ideas

- **Talk through your projects in chat.** Ideas are generated from your conversations — the more Muse knows about what you're working on, the more relevant the cards.
- **Don't hoard cards.** If one isn't for you, long-press and dismiss it. A clean tab makes the good suggestions easier to spot.
- **Act on the timely ones.** Cards tied to deadlines (scholarship applications, expiring offers) won't wait for you — run them while they're fresh.
- **Check the tab when you check the Feed.** Make it part of a quick routine: skim the Feed for what's new, glance at Ideas for what Muse is offering.
- **Say yes to the small ones.** A two-minute idea you actually run teaches you what the tab is good for faster than any tutorial.

## Limitations Worth Knowing

- **You can't create idea cards yourself.** They're generated by Muse, not authored by you. If you know exactly what you want, just ask for it in chat instead.
- **Muse can't dismiss cards for you.** Long-press is the only path, and it's a manual one.
- **Cards expire after about two weeks.** The tab is designed to stay fresh, not to archive every suggestion ever made.
- **Running an idea behaves differently by platform.** Android runs cards in place with live progress; iPhone opens a detail view; the web uses a "Let's do it" button. Same work, slightly different flow.
- **Ideas aren't Goals.** Running an idea does one piece of work. If you want ongoing tracking with check-ins and milestones, that's what the Goals tab is for.

## Common Questions

**Where do the ideas come from?**
Muse generates them from your conversations, goals, and project context. They're proactive suggestions — things Muse thinks it could usefully build or do for you.

**Can I make my own idea card?**
No. Cards are generated by Muse. But anything you'd put on a card, you can just ask for directly in chat.

**Why did an idea disappear?**
It most likely aged out — cards retire after about two weeks. Or you (or someone with your phone) dismissed it with a long-press.

**Can Muse dismiss an idea for me?**
No. There's no way for the assistant to remove a card. Long-press the card yourself and use the dismiss control.

**I tapped an idea on my iPhone and nothing seemed to run. Is it broken?**
Not broken — that's the expected behavior. On iPhone, tapping opens the idea's detail view rather than running it inline. On Android, cards can run in place with live progress.

**What's the difference between Ideas and the Feed?**
The Feed is a stream of posts Muse writes for you to read — briefings, summaries, updates. Ideas are offers of work — things Muse will build or do if you say yes. Some Feed posts are built from ideas and let you kick off the work right from the post.

**Are Ideas available on WhatsApp?**
No. Like the Feed, the Ideas tab only exists in the Muse app and on the web at muse.ai.

## Related Meta Muse Features

- **Goals** — turn a one-off idea into a tracked, ongoing objective with milestones and check-ins.
- **Feed** — some Feed posts are built from ideas; it's also where Muse's written-for-you briefings live.
- **Library** — everything an idea produces (documents, files, images) ends up collected here.
- **Chat** — the fallback for everything: ask for the work directly, refine an idea, or follow up on one you ran.
- **Search** — can't find a result from an idea you ran last week? Search across chats, files, and goals.
