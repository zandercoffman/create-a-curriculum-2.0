# Chatting with Meta Muse

Chat is where everything happens. The Feed is for reading, Ideas are for offers, Goals are for tracking — but the conversation itself is where you ask, commission, refine, and follow up. If you only learn one part of Meta Muse, learn this one: everything else connects back here.

It's a normal conversation, like texting a capable assistant who remembers your projects, your preferences, and your open threads. Type, talk, or send a photo — say what you want, and Muse does the work or asks the one question it actually needs. Here's how to use it well.

## What Chat Is — and What It Isn't

Chat is the main conversation with your Muse: the place you ask questions, hand off work, share files and photos, and pick up threads from yesterday. Every other tab feeds into it or out of it.

A few distinctions that keep things clear:

- **Chat is the fallback for everything.** Anything you'd put on another tab, you can just ask for here: "track my interview prep" (Goals), "find me scholarship options" (an Idea-style ask), "what did we decide about my resume?" (memory + files).
- **It's not just Q&A.** Asking "what's the capital of Arizona?" works, but chat's real power is commissioned work: research it, build it, draft it, check it, summarize it.
- **It's not one endless thread.** Your **Main chat** is the primary ongoing conversation, but **side chats** give separate topics their own durable threads with their own context.
- **It's not the whole app.** The Feed, Ideas, Goals, Library, and Search tabs each do something chat doesn't do as well — browse them when you want to read, pick, track, retrieve, or find.

## What You Can Do in Chat

Here's what a conversation can actually carry:

- **Ask anything** — quick facts, explanations, advice, opinions. Short questions get short answers.
- **Commission work** — "research this," "draft my cover letter," "build me a workout plan," "summarize this PDF." Describe the outcome; Muse handles the steps.
- **Share photos and files** — screenshots, documents, images of things you're working on. Muse can read them and work from what's in them.
- **Talk instead of type** — dictate messages or send voice notes when that's easier than typing.
- **Refine in place** — "make that shorter," "try a bolder version," "use simpler words." Follow-ups in the same thread keep all the context.
- **Manage the other tabs by asking** — "remind me Thursday at 7 PM," "add a goal for my reading streak," "what ideas do you have for my portfolio?"
- **Pick up old threads** — "where did we leave off on my resume?" Muse searches your history and tells you.

## Starting a Conversation

1. Open the Muse app on your phone, or go to [muse.ai](https://muse.ai) in your browser.
2. Open the **Chat** tab — your Main chat is right there, with your history intact.
3. Type in the message box at the bottom (or use the microphone to dictate), and send. That's the whole onboarding.

> ⚠️ **Heads up:** The full app — Chat plus the Feed, Ideas, Goals, Library, and Search tabs — lives in the Muse app and on the web at muse.ai. If you connect **WhatsApp**, you can chat with Muse there too, but WhatsApp gives you the core conversation only; the tabs stay in the app.

## Main Chat vs. Side Chats

Not every topic belongs in one thread. That's what side chats are for:

- **Main chat** — your primary, ongoing conversation. Day-to-day questions, quick tasks, and anything that doesn't need its own space live here.
- **Side chats** — separate threads you create when a topic deserves its own durable home: a big project, a trip you're planning, an ongoing learning thread. Each side chat keeps its own transcript and context, so a long project thread never buries your daily chat.

A practical rule: if you'd give it a name ("interview prep," "kitchen remodel," "CIS 137 project"), it probably deserves a side chat. If it's a one-off question, the Main chat is fine.

## Chatting Outside the App

- **In the app and on the web**, you get the complete Muse: chat plus every tab, voice, file uploads, and interactive cards.
- **On WhatsApp**, you get Muse in your pocket inside a messenger you already use — the conversation works the same, but the Feed, Ideas, Goals, and Library tabs aren't there. For anything tab-shaped, open the app or muse.ai.

## Tips for Better Chats

- **Say what you want, not just the topic.** "Help me write a cover letter for a McDonald's crew position, friendly tone, under 200 words" beats "cover letter."
- **Give the details that change the answer.** Dates, deadlines, constraints, and what "good" looks like. Muse will ask if something's missing — answering once is faster than three rounds of guessing.
- **Keep one thread per topic.** Follow-ups belong in the thread where the work started. Starting over in a fresh message loses the context you already built.
- **Correct plainly.** "That's not what I meant — I wanted X" is the fastest fix there is. You don't need to be polite about it, just specific.
- **Let Muse do the remembering.** "What did we decide about my resume summary?" and "find that link you sent me last week" are legitimate requests — that's what history and Search are for.
- **Talk through your projects out loud.** The more Muse hears about what you're working on, the better its Ideas get and the sharper its check-ins become. Chat is what feeds everything else.

## Limitations Worth Knowing

- **Muse can't see what you don't share.** It knows your conversations, your connected accounts, and what you've told it — not what's on your mind right now. When in doubt, say it.
- **Long work takes a moment.** Research, builds, and multi-step jobs run in the background; you'll get the result when it's ready rather than instantly.
- **Some actions need your go-ahead.** Sending messages, posting, buying, or deleting things will pause for your approval first. That's by design.
- **Side chats don't share context automatically.** A detail from your Main chat may not be in a side chat's thread — mention it again if it matters there.
- **WhatsApp is chat-only.** No tabs, no Feed, no Ideas cards there — just the conversation.

## Common Questions

**Is chat the same as the other tabs?**
No. Chat is the conversation where work happens. The tabs are specialized views: Feed (reading), Ideas (offers), Goals (tracking), Library (your files), Search (finding things).

**What's the difference between the Main chat and a side chat?**
The Main chat is your primary ongoing thread. A side chat is a separate named thread for a topic that deserves its own space and context — a project, a plan, a learning arc.

**Can I use voice instead of typing?**
Yes — dictate messages or send voice notes when that's easier. Muse handles both the same as typed text.

**Can Muse see my photos and files?**
If you send or upload them, yes. Screenshots, documents, and photos you share in chat become part of what Muse can read and work from.

**Does Muse remember everything I say?**
It keeps your conversation history and saves durable facts and preferences to memory, so you don't repeat yourself. If something shouldn't be kept, you can ask it to forget.

**Can I chat with Muse on WhatsApp?**
Yes, once connected — you get the core conversation there. The tabs (Feed, Ideas, Goals, Library) stay in the app and on muse.ai.

## Related Meta Muse Features

- **Feed** — posts Muse writes for you: briefings, summaries, updates. Born from your chats, read on your time.
- **Ideas** — proactive offers of work from Muse, generated from your conversations. Run one with a tap.
- **Goals** — ongoing objectives Muse tracks with you: habits, projects, deadlines.
- **Library** — everything chat produces (documents, files, images), collected in one place.
- **Search** — find anything across your chats, files, and goals when you can't remember which thread it was in.
