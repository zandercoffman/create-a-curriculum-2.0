# Using the Feed in Meta Muse

The Feed is your personal newspaper inside Meta Muse — a stream of short posts that Muse writes just for you, in the background, on a regular schedule. Open the tab in the morning and you'll find a briefing shaped around your interests, your work, and whatever you've been talking to Muse about lately.

This isn't a social timeline and it isn't an RSS reader. Muse doesn't repost articles from sites you subscribe to, and no ranking algorithm decides what you see. Every post is authored fresh, for one reader — you — based on instructions you write in plain language.

Tell it what you care about, how you like it delivered, and when. Skim the headlines over coffee, tap **Discuss** on anything worth a deeper conversation, and reshape the whole thing whenever your interests change. Here's how it all works.

## What the Feed Is — and What It Isn't

Think of the Feed as Muse doing homework for you overnight. You describe what a useful briefing looks like, and Muse researches and writes the posts — drawing on the live web, social platforms, your connected accounts (like email and calendar), and the context it already has about you from your conversations.

A few things that make it different from feeds you're used to:

- **Every post is written, not aggregated.** Muse composes each post itself and cites links exactly as its research surfaced them. There's no way to point it at a site, an RSS feed, or a source list and have it repost from there.
- **There's no like-driven ranking.** Posts appear in time-based editions (like "Friday afternoon"), not sorted by an engagement algorithm.
- **It learns your taste — from what you do, not just what you read.** Reacting to posts and discussing them with Muse shapes future editions. Merely opening a post doesn't count.

## What the Feed Can Do

Here's what that looks like in practice:

- **Write you a morning briefing** — top headlines in your industry, what's on your calendar today, and one thing worth knowing, all in a five-minute skim.
- **Track topics you care about** — new research on a hobby, price drops on something you're watching, developments in a field you're studying.
- **Connect the dots across your life** — because Muse can see your connected accounts, a post can reference your actual schedule or inbox, not just generic news.
- **Adjust to your rhythm** — the more you chat with Muse and react to posts, the more posts get written. Active readers get fuller editions, including multi-post daily briefings.

## Opening the Feed

1. Open the Muse app on your phone, or go to [muse.ai](https://muse.ai) in your browser.
2. Look for the **Feed** tab in the main navigation — its icon looks like a small stack of pages.
3. Tap it. You'll see your posts grouped under time headings like **Friday afternoon**, each with a headline, a short summary, and sometimes an image.

## How Posts Get Written

You don't write the posts — you write the *instructions*, and Muse does the rest. Those instructions are a plain-text prompt sometimes called "the brief." Everything in your Feed flows from it.

Generation happens on a schedule controlled by the system, not by you or by Muse. A few things worth knowing about the rhythm:

- **New posts arrive quietly.** There's no push notification and no chat message announcing them — they simply appear in the Feed tab.
- **Opening the Feed can nudge one along.** If you open the Feed (or pull to refresh on mobile) and the last generation started more than ten minutes ago, one fresh post may be added. Refreshing twice in a row won't force more.
- **Your activity level matters.** How much you chat and read affects how often posts get written — active readers get more.
- **When you first start out**, the Feed opens with a fixed set of intro posts explaining the app — the Feed itself, Ideas, Goals, the Library, side chats. Those are shipped with the app and aren't based on anything about you.

## Making the Feed Yours: Editing the Feed Instructions

This is the single most important thing you can do with the Feed. The instructions are short, editable anytime, and every change applies to *future* posts.

1. Open the **Feed** tab.
2. Tap the **sliders icon** in the top-right corner of the Feed header.
3. A dialog titled **Feed instructions** opens, showing your current prompt in a text box. It reads something like: *"Your feed is powered by the instructions below. Any edits you make to this prompt will apply to future posts on the feed."*
4. Rewrite the instructions to describe exactly what you want.
5. Tap **Save**.

Two things happen when you save a real change: your instructions update, and a fresh post starts generating in the background to reflect them. It runs behind the scenes and lands on top of your Feed when it's ready — give it a little time rather than expecting it instantly. Re-saving the exact same text does nothing.

If your Feed is brand new, you may instead see a prompt card with an **Edit** dialog and a **Generate** button. Same idea: write your instructions, generate, and from then on the editor lives in the Feed header.

### Writing instructions that actually work

Be specific about *what*, *when*, and *how*. A vague prompt gets you a vague newspaper. Compare:

- Vague: *"Tell me interesting stuff."*
- Better: *"Every weekday morning, give me a 5-minute briefing: the three biggest tech headlines, one thing from my calendar today I should prepare for, and one practical fitness tip. Keep each item to two or three sentences — skimmable, no clickbait."*

More examples to steal from:

- *"I'm training for a half marathon. Post twice a week: one evidence-based training tip and one recovery or nutrition note. Skip generic motivation."*
- *"Keep me posted on AI regulation news and anything affecting small business owners in Arizona. Short summaries with source links. Weekday evenings only."*
- *"Once a week, recommend one nonfiction book related to what I've been asking you about, with a two-sentence pitch for why I'd like it."*

> 💡 **Tip:** You can be very specific — Meta's own walkthrough suggests things like "finance news in the morning and nonfiction book recommendations in the evening." Timing, topics, tone, and length are all fair game.

You don't have to use the dialog, either. Just tell Muse in chat — *"less crypto, more Formula 1"* — and it will update your Feed instructions for you.

## Working With Individual Posts

Each post has its own small set of controls:

- **Heart** — react to a post you like. This is genuine taste feedback: it teaches the Feed what to write more of.
- **Discuss** — starts a chat message carrying the post, so you can ask follow-up questions or dig deeper. (It's a private conversation with Muse, not a public thread.)
- **••• menu** — **Move up**, **Move down**, **Move to top**, **Why I created this** (on posts that carry an explanation), and **Delete**.

You can also ask Muse in chat to tidy things up — *"delete the posts about crypto"* or *"move the marathon tips to the top"* — and it will handle it. And if you're hunting for something older, ask Muse to search your past posts.

## Tips for Getting Better Results

- **Start with one clear briefing**, not ten topics at once. You can always broaden it later.
- **React and discuss.** The Feed learns from your hearts and your follow-up questions far more than from silent scrolling.
- **Name your sources of truth.** "Summarize with links to the original reporting" gets you checkable posts instead of vibes.
- **Set a tone.** "Clear, direct, and grounded — no clickbait, no culture-war hot takes" works as well for news as it does for anything else.
- **Revisit the instructions monthly.** Your interests drift; a two-minute edit keeps the Feed from going stale.
- **Connect your accounts.** The Feed gets dramatically more useful once Muse can see your calendar, email, and other services — that's what turns generic news into *your* briefing. (Nothing is connected until you connect it yourself in Settings.)

## Limitations Worth Knowing

- **You can't set the schedule.** Generation timing belongs to the system. The instructions control *what* gets written, not *when*.
- **Edits only affect future posts.** Already-published posts don't rewrite themselves when you change the instructions.
- **There's no off switch.** No toggle turns Feed generation on or off. If you'd rather not see it, you can shrink the instructions down to almost nothing, delete existing posts, or simply ignore the tab — reading and searching old posts still works.
- **It's not a news aggregator.** You can't subscribe it to websites, newsletters, or RSS feeds, and it won't repost full articles — it writes original summaries with links.

> 🔒 **Privacy:** The Feed can draw on your connected accounts and your conversation context to personalize posts. The default intro posts that ship with the app use none of your data. Review what's connected under Settings before asking the Feed to get personal.

## Common Questions

**Is the Feed available on WhatsApp?**
No. WhatsApp gives you the core Muse conversation, but the Feed tab only exists in the Muse app and on the web at muse.ai.

**I edited my instructions — why hasn't anything changed?**
A real edit kicks off a fresh post in the background. It starts right away but finishes later, with no announcement — check back in a bit rather than expecting it instantly.

**Does the Feed just repost articles from around the web?**
No. Every post is written fresh for you. It researches the live web and social platforms, then composes an original summary and cites its sources with links.

**Can I turn the Feed off?**
There's no toggle for that. Your options are to narrow the instructions, delete posts you don't want, or just not open the tab.

**Why am I seeing posts about something I never asked for?**
The Feed also picks up signals from your recent main-chat conversations and your reactions. If something's off-topic, delete the post and tighten your instructions — or just tell Muse in chat what to change.

**Do I have to write the instructions myself?**
No. Describe what you want in chat — *"give me a weekday morning tech briefing"* — and Muse will write the instructions for you.

## Related Meta Muse Features

- **Goals** — turn something the Feed surfaces into a longer-term objective Muse helps you work toward.
- **Ideas** — proactive suggestions from Muse based on your conversations; a good companion when the Feed sparks a new interest.
- **Library** — everything Muse creates for you, collected in one place — handy when a Feed post leads to a document or plan.
- **Search** — can't find a post from last week? Search across your chats, files, and goals to track it down.
- **Connectors** — link Gmail, Google Calendar, and other services so the Feed can personalize around your real schedule.
