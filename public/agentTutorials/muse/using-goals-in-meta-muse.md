# Using Goals in Meta Muse

Goals is the tab where Muse helps you *stay on track*. Instead of a suggestion you run once, a Goal is an ongoing objective Muse tracks with you over time — a daily habit, a multi-week project, a deadline you're working toward — with milestones, check-ins, and progress you can see at a glance.

Each goal shows up as an entry with checkboxes for its steps or milestones. Open one, and Muse knows the full context: what you're working toward, what's done, and what's still open. It's the closest thing in the app to having an assistant who remembers what you committed to and keeps you honest about it. Here's how to use it well.

## What Goals Are — and What They Aren't

Goals are ongoing objectives you set and Muse helps you track. They describe outcomes you're working toward over days or weeks, not tasks you finish in one sitting.

A few distinctions that keep things clear:

- **Goals are commitments, not proposals.** Unlike Ideas, which Muse pitches to you, Goals start with you — you decide the objective, Muse helps you reach it.
- **They're not a to-do list you write once and forget.** A goal stays alive: Muse checks in on it, tracks milestones, and surfaces progress.
- **They're not Ideas.** An Idea is a single piece of work on offer right now ("want me to build this?"). A Goal is the longer arc that work might serve ("read one chapter a day, every day").
- **They come in two flavors.** The tab shows your personal **Goals** (enduring objectives like a daily reading habit) and a **Tracking** list (concrete projects and commitments Muse is keeping an eye on for you, like interview prep or a workshop series).

## What Goals Can Do

Here's what that looks like in practice:

- **Hold a daily habit** — "Read one chapter a day." Muse tracks the streak and checks in on it, so the habit survives busy weeks.
- **Carry a multi-week project** — interview prep, a workshop series, a final project submission. Milestones break the big outcome into steps you can check off.
- **Keep deadlines in view** — resume workshops, class submissions, applications. The goal keeps the date attached to the work.
- **Bundle the loose ends** — instead of remembering ten open threads yourself, the Tracking list holds them: the Notion page you're building, the guide you commissioned, the build in progress.
- **Check in with you** — Muse surfaces goal briefings and nudges tied to what you're tracking, so progress (or drift) doesn't go unnoticed.

## Opening the Goals Tab

1. Open the Muse app on your phone, or go to [muse.ai](https://muse.ai) in your browser.
2. Look for the **Goals** tab in the main navigation.
3. Tap it. You'll see your goals grouped into sections, each entry with checkboxes for its steps or milestones.

## The Two Lists: Goals and Tracking

The tab is split into two sections, and the difference matters:

- **Goals** (blue dot) — your personal, enduring objectives. These are the long arcs: habits, practices, ongoing commitments. Example: *Read one chapter a day*.
- **Tracking** (green dot) — concrete projects and commitments Muse is actively tracking for you. These usually have an end state: interview prep, a workshop series, a project submission, a page you're building. Example: *McDonald's Crew Team Member interview prep*, *CIS 137 final project submission*.

If the Tracking list gets long, a **"Show more"** control expands it so the tab stays scannable instead of overwhelming.

## Creating a Goal

1. Scroll to **Create a goal** at the bottom of the tab.
2. Pick a category that fits — **Health**, **Relationships**, **Finance**, **Career**, **Interests**, and more. The category is just a starting point; it shapes the setup questions Muse asks.
3. Describe the outcome you want, in your own words. Name the result, not the task list — "be ready for my Friday interview" beats "do interview stuff."
4. Muse sets up the tracking: milestones or steps with checkboxes, any dates that matter, and a check-in rhythm.

## Working a Goal

A goal isn't a poster on the wall; it's a working surface. Here's the day-to-day:

- **Check off steps as you finish them.** Tapping a checkbox marks that milestone done. The rest stay visible, so you always know what's next.
- **Talk to Muse about it in chat.** "I finished the first mock interview" or "I read today's chapter" — Muse logs the progress against the goal.
- **Watch for check-ins.** Muse surfaces briefings and nudges tied to your goals: reminders before a deadline, a prompt when a habit streak is at risk, a summary of where a project stands.
- **Adjust mid-course.** Goals change — a deadline moves, a scope narrows. Just say so in chat and Muse updates the tracking.

## Completing, Pausing, or Removing a Goal

- **Completing:** when the outcome is reached — the interview is done, the project submitted — mark the goal complete. It moves out of your active list.
- **Pausing:** if life intervenes, say so. A paused goal stops its check-ins without losing your progress.
- **Removing:** if a goal no longer fits, remove it. Like dismissing an idea card, this is yours to do — Muse won't silently delete your goals.

## Tips for Getting Better Use Out of Goals

- **Name the outcome, not the activity.** "Submit my CIS 137 final project" tracks better than "work on school."
- **One goal per outcome.** Ten vague goals are harder to track than three sharp ones. Let the Tracking list hold the projects; keep the Goals list for the arcs that matter most.
- **Give every goal a date or a rhythm.** A goal with no "by when" or "how often" drifts. A deadline or a daily/weekly cadence is what turns an intention into something Muse can actually check in on.
- **Report progress in plain words.** You don't need a special format — "did day 3 of the reading streak" is enough for Muse to log it.
- **Review the tab weekly.** A two-minute skim of Goals and Tracking each week catches the thing that quietly slipped.
- **Let Muse do the remembering.** That's the whole point: offload the "what was I working on?" to the tab, and spend your energy on the work itself.

## Limitations Worth Knowing

- **Goals track the work; they don't do it.** A goal keeps the objective alive and visible, but the doing still happens in chat, in your apps, or in real life.
- **Check-ins depend on context.** Muse can only nudge you about what it knows — if progress happens entirely offline and you never mention it, the goal goes quiet.
- **A goal is only as sharp as its setup.** "Get in shape" gives Muse nothing to track. "Gym 3x a week through December" gives it everything.
- **The tab is yours to curate.** Muse won't retire a stale goal on its own — if an objective is dead, complete it or remove it so the tab stays honest.

## Common Questions

**Where do goals come from?**
You create them — from the "Create a goal" section or by asking in chat. The Tracking list can also grow out of your conversations, when Muse picks up a concrete commitment worth keeping an eye on.

**What's the difference between the Goals and Tracking sections?**
Goals are your enduring personal objectives (habits, practices, long arcs). Tracking holds the concrete projects and commitments with an end state (prep, submissions, builds, deadlines).

**What's the difference between Goals and Ideas?**
Ideas are one-off offers of work Muse pitches to you — you run one and it's done. Goals are ongoing objectives tracked over time. Running an idea can serve a goal, but they're different tools.

**Can Muse create a goal for me?**
It can set one up from your description, but the objective itself is yours to name. Muse won't invent goals you didn't ask for.

**I finished a goal — now what?**
Mark it complete. If the work taught you something worth keeping (a system, a template, a checklist), ask Muse to save that before you close it out.

**Do goals work on WhatsApp?**
The Goals tab itself only exists in the Muse app and on the web at muse.ai — same as Ideas and the Feed. But you can always talk about your goals in chat, wherever you're chatting.

## Related Meta Muse Features

- **Ideas** — one-off offers of work from Muse; running an idea can knock out a milestone inside a goal.
- **Feed** — some Feed posts are goal briefings: progress summaries, deadline reminders, check-ins on your habits.
- **Library** — everything a goal produces (documents, files, images) ends up collected here.
- **Chat** — the fallback for everything: report progress, adjust a goal, or set one up from scratch.
- **Search** — can't find a result tied to a goal from last month? Search across chats, files, and goals.
